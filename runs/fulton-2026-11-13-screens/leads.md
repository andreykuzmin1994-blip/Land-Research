## How to read this

- A: every check came back clear. The zoning path is by right or likely, a prototype building envelope fits on the open ground, a detention pond fits beside it, the parcel has public road frontage, the slope and soils readings came back and are under their limits, the parcel map agrees with the tax record, and none of these was found: the same owner built next door, a listed contamination site on the parcel, a priced deed in the last few years.
- B: exactly one of those was found or could not be checked (a failed service counts as not checked, never as clear). C: two or more. Blocked: the zoning path is against industrial use.
- The grade is a fixed rule over this screen's own checks, each with the limits given beside it in the brief: "found" is what the screen found, not proof (a missing building fit is a lower bound; frontage is measured to a road centreline; soils are map-unit level). It orders a reading list. It is not a score, a screen or a recommendation, and it does not change the ranking.
- Still not checked by anything here: utilities and their capacity, zoning conditions, easements other than conservation easements, curb cuts, whether the parcel is for sale, and who is behind a company owner. Each brief lists its own next steps.
